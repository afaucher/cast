Menu style options: four "paper trade-business" systems

Each option is a full vocabulary that maps every screen we have (and the business screens we will need) to one printed object. Each is shown on the same five screens: main menu, job board, supply-house shop, cosmetics, settings. Backgrounds are real in-engine prototype captures from scenes/backdrop_lab.tscn (see backdrops.html). The write-up with the screen inventory and the build plan is docs/design/ui-theme-options.md.

Screens are drawn at 1280×720 and scaled down. The yellow highlighter or red grease-pencil mark is the keyboard and controller focus in every option.

Option A

Desk of objects every screen is a different physical thing

The concept image, extended: the clipboard holds the main menu, job tickets are pinned to a corkboard, the shop is a mail-order catalog page, cosmetics are paint-swatch fans and a uniform catalog, settings is an equipment checklist. Each screen gets the object that fits it best.

Works

  • The most charm per screen, and the closest to the concept.
  • Each object explains its screen without words: a catalog is a shop.
  • Strong screenshots and store-page material.

Strains

  • Every screen is bespoke art and layout; new screens cost the most.
  • Focus looks different on every object, which makes controller navigation harder to learn.
  • Rotated, layered objects fight scrolling and 800p (Steam Deck) readability.
  • The corkboard hides the 3D backdrop.

Option B Recommended

Carbon-copy forms one form system, many forms

Every screen is a pre-printed trade form: a numbered "FORM 27-B" header, a ruled grid of boxed fields in one spot ink, typed values, and carbon colors. Each screen type gets its own color (white office copy, canary dispatch, pink shop, goldenrod settings, green crew). Short lists clip onto the concept's clipboard. Job offers are a 3-part tear-off set. Focus is always the same yellow highlighter band plus a red grease-pencil tick, so it reads the same on every screen.

Works

  • Rows, cells, tables and checkboxes are exactly what Godot Containers and StyleBoxes do well. One Theme covers about 90% of it.
  • Dense screens (settings, shop tables, invoice) are natural for a form.
  • One focus language for mouse, keyboard and controller.
  • Scrolls cleanly: a form can be a long sheet.
  • The invoice we already have is the same family.

Strains

  • Less whimsical than A or D. The charm has to come from copy, stamps and colors.
  • Risk of "spreadsheet" if the spot ink and typed fonts are not kept strict.
  • Bright paper over a dark scene: keep the paper warm off-white, not white, and leave the right side of the screen to the backdrop.

Option C

Dispatch printout the rig prints everything on tractor-feed paper

The dispatch rig is a cheap dot-matrix printer. Menus feed out of it on green-bar continuous paper with sprocket holes. Focus is a yellow highlighter swipe, and a choice is circled in pen. Job offers are tear-off printouts. The shop is a parts list, and cosmetics are a color-code printout with paint chips stapled on.

Works

  • Lists scroll for free, because the paper simply feeds.
  • Monospace makes layout trivial and very cheap to build: one font, one paper style.
  • "The platform printed it" fits the gig-app satire.
  • Feed-in animations are cheap and fun.

Strains

  • Cold and "computer": less trade-business and less magic.
  • Pixel fonts at small sizes hurt Steam Deck readability, and they rarely cover CJK or Cyrillic.
  • No color for cosmetics, so they lean on stapled props.
  • Monospace text widths blow up with localization (German).

Option D

Mail-order flyer yellow pages, coupons and catalog spreads

Loud two-color newsprint (spot red and black on cream) with halftones, starbursts, dashed coupons and slab headlines, like the concept's "classified ad" boxes. The main menu is a full-page ad, the job board is the classifieds page, the shop is a supply-house sale flyer, and cosmetics is "van wraps, as seen on cable".

Works

  • The funniest and most branded option, with the strongest marketing look.
  • The shop and cosmetics are perfect fits: sale tags, price bursts, product tiles.
  • Flat two-color print is cheap to render: no rotations, no layering.

Strains

  • Busy: halftones and bursts compete with content, and readability suffers on the Deck.
  • Settings and voice setup have no natural flyer form ("mail-in coupon" is a stretch).
  • The novelty wears thin as the frame for every screen; better as an accent.
  • Heavy slab display fonts need careful fallbacks for localization.

Recommendation

Option B, carbon-copy forms, with the clipboard as its holder

Why: it is the only option that handles every screen we have and every screen we plan: short lists, dense settings, live voice meters, comparable job offers, scrolling shop tables, the existing invoice and a co-op time card. It does that with one Godot Theme and five or six reusable components, and with one focus language for mouse, keyboard and controller. It still delivers the concept's clipboard and classified ads, because the main menu is a form on a clipboard with ad boxes at the bottom.

Borrow from the others, as accents only: D's sale bursts and price tags on shop items and cosmetics; A's corkboard sticky notes as crew annotations ("not the guru again"); C's receipt tape for the in-game damage ticker. The frame stays the form system.

Fonts (all OFL unless noted; none downloaded yet): Barlow Condensed (printed form labels), Courier Prime (typed values), Permanent Marker (grease pencil, Apache 2.0) or Caveat (OFL) for marks, and Black Ops One (rubber stamps). CJK and Cyrillic need Noto fallbacks in the Theme.
